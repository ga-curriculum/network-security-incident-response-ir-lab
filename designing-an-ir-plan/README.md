## Making a plan

Now that you've chosen your attack method and understood its potential impact, it's time to start designing your incident response plan. An effective IR plan is like a roadmap - it guides your team through the complex process of detecting, analyzing, containing, and recovering from a security incident.

## Components of an IR plan

A comprehensive IR plan typically includes the following components:

1. **Preparation:** This involves establishing an IR team, defining roles and responsibilities, setting up communication channels, and ensuring the necessary tools and resources are in place.

2. **Identification:** This is the process of detecting and confirming that an incident has occurred. It involves monitoring systems, analyzing alerts, and assessing the scope and severity of the incident.

3. **Containment:** Once an incident is identified, the next step is to contain the damage and prevent further spread. This might involve isolating affected systems, blocking malicious traffic, or temporarily shutting down services.

4. **Eradication:** After containing the incident, the focus shifts to removing the threat from the environment. This could include patching vulnerabilities, removing malware, or resetting compromised credentials.

5. **Recovery:** This is the process of restoring systems and services to their pre-incident state. It might involve restoring from backups, rebuilding servers, or updating configurations.

6. **Lessons learned:** After the incident is resolved, conducting a post-incident review is crucial. This is an opportunity to assess what went well, what could be improved, and what preventative measures can be put in place to avoid similar incidents in the future.

## Tailoring your plan

As you design your IR plan, keep your chosen attack method in mind. Consider:

- What specific tools or techniques might be needed to detect and analyze this type of attack?

- What unique containment or eradication steps might be required?

- How can you prioritize recovery efforts to minimize business impact?

Remember, the goal is not just to have a generic IR plan but one specifically tailored to defend against your chosen attack method.

## Documenting your plan

As you develop your plan, document each component clearly and concisely. Your IR plan should be a living document that your entire team can easily understand and follow.

Consider using flowcharts, checklists, or decision trees to make your plan more visual and actionable. The more clear and practical your plan is, the more effective it will be in guiding your team through a high-pressure incident.

By the end of this exercise, you should have a comprehensive draft of your IR plan.

Remember, a well-crafted IR plan is not just a paperwork exercise - it's a critical tool in your cybersecurity arsenal. So put your strategic thinking cap on, and let's design a plan to make those attackers think twice!
