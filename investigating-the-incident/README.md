**Learning objective:** By the end of this lesson, students will be able to apply the incident response plan to investigate a simulated incident, gathering evidence and analyzing the attack.

Alright, it's time to put your IR plan into action! In this exercise, you'll be simulating an incident investigation based on your chosen attack method.

## Scenario

Imagine that your company's intrusion detection system has just triggered an alert indicating that your chosen attack method has been detected on the network. As the lead incident responder, it's your job to investigate this incident and gather as much information as possible.

## Following Your IR Plan

Start by referring to your IR plan. What are the first steps you should take in the identification phase?
This might involve:

- Verifying the alert and determining the scope of the incident

- Notifying the appropriate team members and stakeholders

- Initiating documentation of the incident

As you move into the containment and eradication phases, consider:

- What systems or segments of the network need to be isolated?

- What tools or techniques can you use to analyze the attack and identify the root cause?

- What steps need to be taken to remove the threat and fortify defenses?

## Gathering Evidence

Throughout your investigation, it's crucial to gather and preserve evidence. This might include:

- Network logs and traffic captures
- System images and memory dumps
- Malware samples and indicators of compromise

Be sure to handle evidence carefully and maintain a clear chain of custody. This will be important for any legal or regulatory proceedings that may follow the incident.

## Analyzing the Attack

As you gather evidence, start piecing together the story of what happened. Consider:

- What was the initial vector of the attack?
- What systems or data were targeted?
- What was the attacker's motive?
- How long was the attacker present in the system before detection?

Try to reconstruct the timeline of the attack and understand the attacker's tactics, techniques, and procedures (TTPs). This analysis will be crucial for preventing similar attacks in the future.

## Documenting Your Findings

As you conduct your investigation, keep detailed notes and documentation. This should include:

- A timeline of the incident and response activities
- Evidence collected and analysis performed
- Key findings and conclusions
- Recommendations for recovery and prevention

This documentation will form the basis for your post-incident report and will be invaluable for learning and improvement.

By the end of this exercise, you should have a comprehensive understanding of the simulated incident and the effectiveness of your IR plan.

In the next exercise, we'll focus on using these findings to strengthen your organization's defenses.

Incident investigation is like being a detective - it requires curiosity, attention to detail, and the ability to piece together a puzzle.
