**Learning objective:** By the end of this lesson, students will be able to prepare and deliver a presentation on your incident response plan, highlighting the key steps, rationale, and expected outcomes

## Preparing Your Presentation

Start by outlining the key points you want to cover in your presentation. A good structure might include:

1. **Overview of the Incident**

   - Brief description of the attack method and its potential impact
   - Key findings from your investigation

2. **Incident Response Plan**

   - Overview of the key components of your plan
   - How your plan is tailored to the specific attack method
   - Key roles and responsibilities of the IR team

3. **Incident Investigation**

   - Timeline of the incident and response activities
   - Key pieces of evidence and how they were analyzed
   - Root causes identified

4. **Preventative Measures**

   - Recommended actions to prevent similar incidents
   - How these measures address the root causes
   - Plan for implementation and continuous improvement

5. **Key Learnings and Next Steps**

   - Main takeaways from the incident and response
   - How the IR plan and security posture will be updated based on these learnings
   - Next steps and timeline for implementation

## Crafting Your Slides

Once you have your outline, start creating your presentation slides. Remember:

- Keep text minimal and use visuals to convey your points

- Use clear, concise language that can be understood by both technical and non-technical audiences

- Highlight the most important information and don't get bogged down in minor details

- Use examples and analogies to make complex concepts more relatable

## Practicing Your Delivery

Before presenting, practice your delivery. This will help you:

- Ensure your presentation fits within the allotted time
- Identify any areas that need clarification or simplification
- Practice handling potential questions or objections
- Build your confidence and comfort with the material

Consider practicing in front of a mirror, recording yourself, or presenting to a trusted colleague for feedback.

## Delivering Your Presentation

When it's time to present, remember:

- Speak clearly and at a measured pace
- Make eye contact with your audience
- Use gestures and body language to engage your listeners
- Allow time for questions and discussion

Be prepared to discuss your reasoning behind your IR plan and preventative measures. Be open to feedback and suggestions from your audience.

## Reflecting and Iterating

After your presentation, take time to reflect on the experience.

- What went well?
- What could be improved?
- What questions or feedback did you receive?
- How can you incorporate this learning into future presentations and your ongoing security work?

Remember, presenting your work is not just about sharing information, but also about continuously improving and refining your ideas through dialogue with others.

Congratulations on completing this Network Security Incident Response Lab! Through this experience, you've not only developed technical skills in incident response and prevention, but also valuable skills in communication, critical thinking, and continuous learning.
