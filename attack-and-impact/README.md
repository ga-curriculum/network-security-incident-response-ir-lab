**Learning objective:** By the end of this lesson, students will be able to select a network attack method and understand its potential impact on an organization.

In this hands-on adventure, you'll have the chance to put your cybersecurity skills to the test by developing a comprehensive incident response plan. But before we dive into the action, let's set the stage.

Imagine this scenario: You're the lead security analyst at a thriving e-commerce company. Your team has just discovered that your network has fallen victim to a cyber attack. The clock is ticking, and it's up to you to lead the charge in responding to this incident and preventing future attacks.

## Choosing Your Attack Method

To make this lab as realistic and relevant as possible, you'll start by selecting a network attack method that you want to focus on. This could be one of the methods we've covered in class, such as:

- On-Path attacks
- Denial of Service (DoS) attacks
- Spoofing attacks
- Phishing and Social Engineering
- Malware and Ransomware

Or it could be another network attack method that you're particularly interested in exploring. The key is to choose a method that you want to understand more deeply and feel prepared to defend against.

## Understanding the Potential Impact

Once you've chosen your attack method, take a moment to consider the potential impact it could have on an organization. Ask yourself:

- What systems, data, or services could be compromised by this type of attack?

- What are the potential financial, reputational, and operational consequences of a successful attack?

- How could this type of attack disrupt business continuity and customer trust?

By understanding the stakes involved, you'll be better equipped to develop an effective incident response plan.

## Setting the Stage

For the purposes of this lab, imagine that your chosen attack method has been successfully carried out against your company's network. Your task is to lead the incident response effort, from initial investigation through to post-incident review and prevention planning.

Are you ready to put your skills to the test and defend your network? Let's dive in!
